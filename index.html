Step 1 · slides/*.svs

Start with a whole-slide image

A scanned H&E slide is a gigapixel file. A network cannot take it in one piece, so LENS works on small parts of it. The slide shown is a real example. Its annotated tumor regions appear as yellow outlines in the last step.

Step 2 · preprocessing/tiling.py

Cut it into patches

The slide is tiled at 20× magnification. A tile that is more than half background is skipped, so only tissue moves on. The tiles that survive are the patches.

python preprocessing/tiling.py -s 512 -B 50 -M 20

Step 3 · preprocessing/graph_construction.py

Build the graph

Each kept patch becomes a node, described by a feature vector (1536 numbers from UNI2-h, or 512 from SimCLR). Each node is linked to its 8 nearest patches by position. The result is one adjacency matrix per slide.

adj_s.pt features.pt c_idx.txt

Step 4 · main.py

Sparsify the edges

An edge scoring network gives every edge a Hard-Concrete gate between 0 and 1. An L0 penalty pushes gates toward 0. Its weight rises through warmup (epochs 0 to 15) and ramp (15 to 35), then holds. A density loss steers training to keep 30% of the edges.

Drag the epoch slider under the slide. Edges that fade out are pruned.

--reg-mode l0 --l0-method hard-concrete --target-density 0.30 --warmup-epochs 15 --ramp-epochs 20

Gate values here are generated for illustration, not recorded from a training run. They are built from the tumor outlines, so the pattern is expected here and not learned.

Step 5 · visualize_heatmap.py

Visualize what stayed

Edge weights are averaged per node and painted over the slide with the JET colormap. Red marks strongly connected tissue and blue marks weakly connected tissue. The yellow outlines are the tumor regions annotated on this slide, so you can check whether the red lands on them.

python visualize_heatmap.py --wsi-path slide.svs --patch-info-path c_idx.txt --pruned-adj-path pruned_adj.pt

The weights shown here are illustrative. A real result uses the pruned adjacency saved from a trained model.
